::: cover variant=typographic kicker="Proposal"
A proposal to pool the specialist tools now held separately by four maintenance teams into one library at the central workshop, run as a one-year pilot from February 2027.
:::

# Proposal overview

The electrical, plumbing, carpentry and grounds teams each buy and store their own specialist tools. An inventory in September 2026 counted 412 tools worth about 186,000 in purchase value (sample figures), of which 141 were used fewer than five times in the past year. Expensive items are duplicated: three core drills, two pipe-threading machines, four thermal cameras.

::: keyfigures
- **141 tools** used fewer than five times a year (sample)
  - of 412 in the September 2026 inventory
- **38,000** purchases avoided over two years (sample)
  - duplicate items not replaced when they wear out
- **14 months** payback of the pilot cost (sample)
  - pilot cost 44,000 against avoided purchases
:::

The library would lend the specialist tools from one place through a simple booking system and keep them calibrated; hand tools stay with the teams.

::: callout kind=key title="Decision requested"
Approval at the November planning meeting for a pilot budget of 44,000 (sample) and a start date of 1 February 2027, with a review at the end of the pilot year.
:::

# Current situation

## Tool holdings by team

Each team keeps its own store room and replacement budget, so a technician who needs another team's tool often hires one instead.

Source: facilities asset register and team store counts, September 2026

## Costs of the current arrangement

Duplicate purchases are the largest cost: of 52 specialist tools replaced in three years, 19 duplicated an item another team held in working order. Hire charges for tools in another team's store came to about 7,500 last year, and two of the four thermal cameras were past their calibration date.

# Pilot design

## Library operation

The library occupies the unused stores room at the central workshop. A part-time coordinator issues and checks tools and arranges calibration and repairs. Technicians book through a new screen in the existing work-order system; urgent requests can be collected without a booking during workshop hours.

# Risks and responses

Teams may worry that a tool will not be available when they need it. The pilot keeps one of each frequently used specialist tool with the team that uses it most, and the coordinator reports waiting times monthly. If the median wait for any tool exceeds one working day, a second unit returns to team use.
