# Summary

Customer contacts rose to 48,300 in the third quarter, 6 percent more than in the second, while the median first response time improved from 4.2 to 3.1 hours. The improvement came almost entirely from the chat channel after the September staffing change; email response times did not move. One contact in five was a repeat contact about the same issue within seven days, and half of those repeats traced back to three causes: unclear refund status, password resets that failed on the first attempt, and delivery updates that arrived late.

The review covers volume, response times and repeat contacts in turn, and ends with three changes proposed for the fourth quarter.

# Contact volume

## Volume by channel

Chat overtook email as the largest channel in August and stayed ahead in September. Phone volume fell slightly, continuing the trend of the past year.

Table 1. Contacts by channel (thousands, sample data)

| Channel | Q2 | Q3 | Change (%) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Email | 19.8 | 18.9 | -4.5 |
| Chat | 16.1 | 20.4 | 26.7 |
| Phone | 7.9 | 7.4 | -6.3 |
| Social media | 1.8 | 1.6 | -11.1 |
| Total | 45.6 | 48.3 | 5.9 |

Source: support ticketing system, contacts opened 1 April to 30 September 2026

## Volume by topic

Orders and delivery remained the largest topic at 38 percent of contacts. Account access rose from 14 to 17 percent after the login change of 22 July, which required every customer to confirm their email address once.

# Response times

## First response

The median first response fell from 4.2 to 3.1 hours. Chat answered within 9 minutes at the median, compared with 21 minutes in the second quarter, after two agents moved from email to chat on 1 September. Email stayed at about 7 hours.

Table 2. Median first response time by channel (sample data)

| Channel | Q2 | Q3 | Target |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Email (hours) | 7.1 | 7.0 | 6.0 |
| Chat (minutes) | 21 | 9 | 10 |
| Phone (minutes) | 4.5 | 4.8 | 5.0 |

Source: support ticketing system and telephony logs

## Resolution

Resolution within one working day held at 72 percent. Contacts about refunds took longest, at a median of 2.4 days, because agents waited for payment confirmations from the finance system.

# Repeat contacts

## Causes

One in five customers contacted support again about the same issue within seven days. A sample review of 600 repeat contacts found three causes behind half of them.

::: columns 2
### Refund status

Customers asked again because the refund confirmation email did not say when the money would arrive. These made up 22 percent of reviewed repeats.

### Password resets

The reset link expired after 15 minutes, and many customers opened it later. These made up 17 percent.

<!-- column-break -->

### Late delivery updates

Tracking pages updated once a day, so customers who asked in the morning saw no change. These made up 12 percent.

### Other causes

The remaining half spread over more than thirty causes, none above 4 percent of reviewed repeats.
:::

::: callout kind=key title="Proposed changes for Q4"
1. Add the expected arrival date to refund confirmation emails. 2. Extend password reset links to 60 minutes. 3. Ask the carrier for tracking updates every four hours. The three changes target about half of repeat contacts.
:::

# Next review

The fourth-quarter review will report repeat contacts for each of the three causes separately, so that the effect of each change can be seen. Email response time remains above target and will be reviewed with the staffing plan for January.
