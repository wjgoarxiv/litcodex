# Creative Directions

Choose one visual center of gravity and adapt it to the product. These archetypes are not brand presets.

| Direction | Useful when | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quiet precision | frequent professional tasks | anonymous styling; add a deliberate type or layout motif |
| Editorial narrative | stories, launches, portfolios | poor scanning or reading order |
| Technical instrument | developer, science, operations, finance | decorative signals that imply false telemetry |
| Soft utility | personal, learning, or guided tasks | pill overload and weak contrast |
| Graphic structure | campaigns, events, expressive moments | novelty obscuring controls or reflow |
| Material depth | creative tools and media browsing | compositing cost, clutter, contrast loss |
| Playful systems | onboarding and low-risk exploration | fatigue, childish tone, or uncontrolled motion |
| Minimal monument | focused, premium narratives | hiding information for the sake of space |

Combine at most one primary direction with one accent. State their separate roles; choose when density, type, or interaction posture conflicts.

## Direction card

Record the name and promise; audience and task; typography, palette, spacing, shape, imagery, and interaction posture; responsive behavior; rejected ideas; and accessibility or performance risks. Test it with representative content and states before broad implementation.
