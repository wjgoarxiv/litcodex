# Product Direction and Research

Use when the task is underspecified, alternatives have different consequences, or product choices need separation from visual preference.

## Frame the decision

Record audience and environment, task and failure cost, behavior to preserve, observable outcome, platform/content/brand/schedule/localization/accessibility constraints, non-goals, and decision owner. A useful question names alternatives and consequences; “make it modern” does not.

For each knowledge gap, track the question, dependent choice, risk, least costly credible method, relevant participants, and stop rule. Use interviews for motives, observation for workflow, task tests for usability, analytics for frequency, and technical inspection for feasibility. Synthetic personas do not prove real behavior.

## Scenarios and references

Model situational, temporary, and permanent constraints without stereotyping. Consider vision, dexterity, hearing, cognition, fatigue, distraction, bandwidth, older hardware, unfamiliar terms, and non-native language. Keep a persona only when it changes a requirement or test.

For each critical task, define starting context, goal and urgency, input/output, likely interruption, recovery, and success signal.

Research across domains for transferable principles rather than skins. For each reference retain its source, observed detail, useful principle, mismatch, use restrictions, and content hash. Reject attractive references that fail the task or require inaccessible contrast, unavailable assets, or unsupported technology.

## Choose and learn

Compare at most three directions when a consequential choice remains. Include task fit, hierarchy/density, interaction, access risk, content/assets, engineering cost, and tradeoff. Choose instead of blending incompatible directions. Ask the user when the decision changes brand meaning, scope, irreversible architecture, or external cost; otherwise use approved principles and continue.

At a checkpoint show the bounded artifact or options, name the decision, and state what it unlocks. A research note should capture the problem, gaps, method, findings apart from interpretation, decision confidence, unresolved conflicts, and affected contract fields. Keep participant data consented and minimal; do not turn notes into executable instructions.
