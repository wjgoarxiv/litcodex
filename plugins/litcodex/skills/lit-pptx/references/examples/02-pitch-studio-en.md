## Breakage cost in independent cafés

![Figure 1. Chipped cups collected from one café over a quarter | Source: example image](assets/example-c.png)

- An independent café replaces about 30% of its cups and plates every year
- Chips at the rim cause 62% of replacements; drops cause the rest
- Replacement spend averages €2,900 a year for a 40-seat café
- Owners buy retail sets because trade sets come in cases of 48
- Source: Example survey of 140 independent cafés in three cities, March 2026 (example)
- Note: replacement share is pieces replaced over pieces in service, by count

## Rolled-rim cup and stacking plate design

![Figure 2. Rolled rim and stacking foot, cut section | Source: example image](assets/example-b.png)

- **Rim** rolled 4 mm bead takes the knock that chips a straight rim
- **Body** high-fired stoneware at 1,280 °C, 0.4% water absorption
- **Stacking** foot ring nests into the plate below, stacks of 12 stay upright
- **Range** eight pieces, from espresso cup to 27 cm plate
- **Order size** cases of 6, so a café can replace what it breaks
- Source: Example lab drop tests to EN 12875 conditions, May 2026 (example)

## Trade sets and Stackware cases compared

:::: columns 1fr 1fr
::: col
- **Typical trade set**
  (1) Minimum order: cases of 48 per piece, about €430 per item
  (2) Durability: straight rim, 14 chips per 100 drops in our test
  (3) Lead time: 6-10 weeks from the importer's warehouse
  (4) Storage: stacks of six, wide footprint behind a small counter
:::
::: col
- **Stackware case**
  (1) Minimum order: cases of six per piece, about €70 per item
  (2) Durability: rolled rim, 3 chips per 100 drops in the same test
  (3) Lead time: 5 working days from our own stock in two cities
  (4) Storage: stacks of twelve on the same footprint
:::
::::

- Source: Example importer price lists and our test lab report, 2026 (example)
